# How I think the game works

I read the Almanac. This is what I took from it.

## 1. How do I get more rare seeds?

Rare is the blue grade. Common has no gem, Uncommon is green, Rare is blue, Heirloom is gold. Bio farmer is a skill, not a fifth grade.

Seed you shovel off a plant keeps that plant’s grade. So the cleanest way to farm Rare seed is to get a Rare plant in the first place, then shovel it. Fruit that drops from a tree has its own grade — most Common, some Uncommon, few Rare, almost never Heirloom — so trees can spit a Rare now and then, but it is not the usual path.

Happier plants more often ripen as a better grade. Careful tending and the better-crop skills help. A new plant starts in the middle of happiness. Empty-handed, Careful tending can lift it a little, once, before it is ripe. Good water and feed slowly raise it. Better-crop skills add a little more chance when the plant is happy. So tending and those skills are how you roll more Rare (and Uncommon, and the rare Heirloom) off the bed, and then you shovel that grade.

Shop seed packs are Common unless you learned Trusted seed bank. Each rank of that skill, a pack has some chance to come in higher: 5% Uncommon, 1.2% Rare, 0.2% Heirloom per rank, Heirloom first. That is the shop route to Rare seed. The Almanac does not say who owns Trusted seed bank, only that you learned it.

The Almanac does not say you can buy Rare packs outright, trade seed, or breed grades. It also does not say whether tree fruit that is Rare can be turned into seed, only that dropped fruit has its own grade.

## 2. What are the benefits and negatives with rare and heirloom seeds?

Both sit above Common on price and both are fussier. They are not the same crop with a gem stuck on.

**Rare**

Benefits: Rare fruit is worth twice the usual price at the Market. Vanilla is triple. Faster in the bed.

Negatives: Goes off faster than Common. Fussier still about water and feed (fussier than Uncommon, which is already a bit fussier than Common).

**Heirloom**

Benefits: Heirloom fruit is worth three and a half times Common at the Market. Vanilla is six times.

Negatives: Grows at the Common pace — so you do not get Rare’s speed. Goes off faster than Common. The narrowest water and feed range.

Uncommon is the middle step the Almanac uses to explain fuss: a quarter more at the Market, a little faster, keeps as long as Common, a bit fussier. Rare and Heirloom do not keep like that. Rare and Heirloom fruit go off faster than Common. Uncommon keeps as long as Common. Above 80% freshness the Market pays full price; below that the price falls with the fruit. When freshness hits nothing, the plot becomes Rotten produce. Picked fruit keeps going off in your hand, the house, a Chest, on the ground, and in a fruit box until you Sell all. A Freezer holds it still. Sugar does not go off.

Seed you shovel off a plant keeps that plant’s grade, so planting Rare or Heirloom seed is how you stay on that grade. Happier plants more often ripen as a better grade, so a Rare seed is not a locked Rare fruit — happiness and the better-crop skills still matter. The Almanac does not say whether a Rare or Heirloom plant can ripen down a grade.

Vanilla is the exception on price for both: triple instead of double for Rare, six times instead of three and a half for Heirloom. The Almanac does not say vanilla grows or spoils differently from other Rare or Heirloom crops.

## 3. What are skills and when do I get them, and what general things do they affect?

The farm is three people. You garden, your husband researches, your daughter runs the Market. Skills are not research. You spend them on Family, the overlay from the HUD: You the Gardener, Husband on Research, Daughter on Market.

A day has four parts: sunrise, day, sunset, twilight. There is no night. When twilight ends, the recap opens: stipend, tax, what you harvested and lost, research that finished. Play waits until you dismiss it. Dismissing starts the next day and gives You, Husband, and Daughter one skill point each. That is when you get them — one point each, every recap, when you dismiss.

Offers stay until that member picks. Points do not move between people. You cannot dump garden points into Market or the other way around.

What they affect, generally:

- **Yours** help the garden: happier plants, better grades, faster walking. Careful tending can lift happiness a little, once, empty-handed, before ripe. Better-crop skills add a little more chance when the plant is happy. Trusted seed bank is how shop packs stop being only Common.
- **His** make Research faster and tools cheaper. He can run only one research job at a time. Starting a job spends money up front and then takes time while you garden. When it finishes, new things appear in the shop and Build, and you can buy more land. Research is how you get new crops, tools, water, machines, and sensors.
- **Hers** change what the Market pays and when it is open. Named on Family: Saleswoman, Őstermelő, Bio farmer, Still good for jam, Clearance sale, Open late, Open 24/7. The Market stays open through sunrise and day. At sunset it needs Open late. At twilight it needs Open 24/7. You can still drop goods off when it is closed; you cannot Sell all until it opens. Still good for jam stops the Market knocking off more than the rank allows. Clearance sale pays $1 for fruit that has gone completely off. Bio farmer is a skill, not a fifth grade.

The Almanac does not list every skill, rank costs, or what Saleswoman, Őstermelő, or Bio farmer actually do beyond “change what you get.”

## 4. What makes a plant unhappy? What happens if I underwater or overwater?

Happiness is how the plant is doing while it grows. It does not change how fast the plant grows — too little water or feed does that.

You need it because happier plants more often ripen as a better grade, and a plant that hits nothing while still growing can die.

A new plant starts in the middle. Empty-handed, Careful tending can lift it a little, once, before it is ripe. Good water and feed slowly raise it.

What drains it, in order: too much water drains it fastest, then too little water, then hungry for feed. So overwatering is the worst for happiness, then underwatering, then no feed.

If happiness hits nothing while the plant is still growing:

- too much water drowns it into Rotten produce
- too little water or no feed leaves a Dead plant

Ripe plants do not die of water or feed; they only spoil. Ripe fruit loses quality while it sits. When freshness hits nothing, the plot becomes Rotten produce.

Underwater also slows growth (too little water or feed does that). Overwater is called out for drowning, not for slowing growth. A Water sensor is one way to watch for wilt and overwater.

The Almanac does not give numbers for the water and feed ranges, how fast happiness moves, or how much fussier Rare and Heirloom are beyond “fussier still” and “the narrowest water and feed range.”
