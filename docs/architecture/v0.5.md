Sprinkler output is per tile per day, not a whole-sprinkler L/s:

## Water grid

`Reservoir` owns `stored`, `drawn`, and derives `rate` / `capacity` from `SOURCE[kind]`. That table is the single hook for future per-type production effects. `drawn` accumulates per producer instance, and `Tap.drawn` per tap, for later pump-only water costs.

An edge holds a `Segment`, never a bare `Edge`: `{ kind: 'pipe' }` or `{ kind: 'valve'; open }`. `flows()` decides conduction; a closed valve blocks its own edge only.

`grid()` is a union-find over vertex keys, rebuilt lazily and invalidated by `dirtyNets()` on every segment, sprinkler, source, tap, or chunk change:

- every conducting segment joins its two vertices
- every source joins **all** corner vertices of **all** its occupied cells

The second rule is the whole fix for corner connection and for source sharing. A pipe touching a source at a single point connects, and two pipe runs meeting the same source become one `Net` instead of each claiming its full output.

A `Net` is `{ sources, sprinklers, taps }`. A sprinkler joins when its vertex is in the graph and at least one incident edge conducts; a tap joins when any of its corners is.

`pull(sources, want)` draws proportionally to `stored` and records into each `Reservoir.drawn`. Every consumer goes through it, so multiple producers and multiple consumers on one net compose without a priority order.

`tickWater` runs after `tickField`: gather into every reservoir, then per net serve each sprinkler `want[i] / total × got`, spread over its growing tiles. Demand comes from `demand()` = growing tiles × `tileRate()`, and `tileRate()` is `SPRINKLER_TILE_RATE` when untuned or the crop's own `waterUsePerSec` when tuned.

`rate(v)` is display-only: what the sprinkler at `v` currently receives, 0 when the net is dry, sourceless, or unreachable. `working()` in the map reads it, so a valve-starved sprinkler has no VFX.

## Object HUD

`world.hud: HudTarget | undefined` plus `openHud` / `closeHud`. `hudSpec(world, target)` maps a target to `{ title, at, options, pick }`; `ObjectHud` renders it anchored to the world position through the camera. A new clickable object is one `HudTarget` member and one spec branch — no new component, no new panel.
