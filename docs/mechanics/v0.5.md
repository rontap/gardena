# v0.5 mechanics

Supersedes [[mechanics/v0.3]] where this file names a replacement. Types and all retuned numbers: [[architecture/v0.5]].

v0.3 soil, weeds, fertilizer and v0.4 happiness stay except below.

## Water sources

Pump, well and rainwater tank each own a tank. They make water every second up to their capacity, and consumers spend from the tank. A source can burst above its production rate for as long as its tank holds out, then falls back to production rate.

Under the Pipes lens a source draws a 45° × across every tile it covers with a water mark in the middle. That is literal: **any corner of any covered tile connects**. A pipe that meets a source at a single point is fed, which it was not before.

A source also bridges. Two pipe runs that both touch the same source are one network, and share that source's output rather than each claiming all of it.

## Rainwater tank

2×1, $20. Gathers on its own — no pump, no pipe run to a source. Slow to fill, large to hold. Piped like any other source.

## Tap

1×1, $10. An access point, not a producer. Fills a bucket fast while the network's tanks hold water. Once they are dry it fills only as fast as the network's sources make water, so a lone pumpjack behind a tap ends up filling at the pumpjack's rate.

A tap with no network, or a network with no source, offers nothing.

## Manual valve

Sits on an edge like pipe, $6. Click it: the gardener walks over and turns it. Blue is open, red is closed.

A closed valve blocks **its own edge only**. If water can reach a sprinkler by any other open route, it does.

## Sprinklers

A sprinkler pours per covered tile, not as one lump. Default is 2.5 L a day into every tile it covers — more than any crop drinks, so an untuned sprinkler overwaters on purpose.

A sprinkler with no water runs no VFX.

## Smart sprinklers

Research. Adds a clickable dial to every sprinkler already placed; it is not a new building. Click a sprinkler to open a vertical list of crops with their daily water use, and pick one. The sprinkler then pours exactly that crop's draw per tile, so the soil holds steady.

Tuning changes the flow, not the soil. A plot already over- or underwatered stays that way until something else moves it.

## Hand watering

A bucket now tops a plot to **1 L plus the plant's own comfort margin**, the top of its green band. Bare, weeded or unplanted soil still tops to 1 L.

A drowning plot takes nothing. A wilting one can take well over 1 L in a single pour.

## Crop thirst

Wheat, tomato and raspberry each drink 0.3 L/day more. Watermelon drinks 0.5 L/day less. Exact figures in [[architecture/v0.5]].
