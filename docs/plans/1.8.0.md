# 1.8.0 — Market pressure and Contracts

Spec. Two halves of one mechanic.

Types are `src/game/sim/market.h.ts`. That file is **typedef only** — every constant is `declare const`, no value is chosen. Formulas and constant names live here; values are a tuning pass, not a spec decision.

Companies locked. See [[#Companies]]. `CompanyId` narrows in 1.8.0-b. Header may stay `string` through 1.8.0-a.

Graduates at implementation into [[mechanics/saturation]], [[mechanics/contracts]], [[ui/contracts]], and numbered entries on [[mechanics/_index]].

## Why both

The stall is *Sell all → one number*, so every quality decision is laundered through one scalar.

- **Saturation is the push.** The unlimited channel's margin declines as you use it.
- **Contracts are the pull.** A capped-volume channel priced off the *clean* baseline.

Equilibrium: contracts take the high-margin volume, overflow goes to the stall at whatever the stall is worth today. Neither half works alone. Saturation alone is a tax; contracts alone are free money.

Two consequences that fall straight out and are law:

1. **Contract deliveries never touch saturation.** Fulfilment would otherwise close the escape valve behind you.
2. **Contract reward is baked at generation from the clean price.** Later saturation does not move an accepted contract's payout.

# Part 1 — Saturation

## Replaces the dead dynamic market

`DYNAMIC_MARKET` in `world.ts` is `false`, and `DYNAMIC_MARKET_DISPLAY` in `ui/market.tsx` is `false`. That system is **deleted**, not extended. Saturation takes its place on `StallGood`.

Removals: `StallGood.offered`, `.market`, `.target`, `.acc`; `rate()` in `stall.ts`; `World.sales`, `World.mktAcc`, `nudgeOffered`, `nudgeOfferedBody`, `retarget`, the `DYNAMIC_MARKET` const and every guard on it; `Act.nudgeOffered` and its `Cmd` arm; `SaveStallGood.offered` / `.market` / `.target` / `.acc`; `DynamicMarketRows` and `DynamicStallRow`; `StallSale`.

Addition: `StallGood.sat: number`, `0..1`, starts 0.

## Model

Saturation accrues in **dollars**, not units. One constant does the work: `SAT_DEPTH` is the clean revenue that takes a good from 0 to fully saturated.

```
mul(sat, good) = 1 - (1 - SAT_FLOOR[good]) * sat
```

`SAT_FLOOR` is per-good, floored at 0.25 for the worst case you named (−75%). Whether it is authored per-good or derived from a coarser grouping is a `defs` question, not a type question — the header takes the per-good map either way.

Two things want different floors and this is the knob for it: shallow floors protect the opening hours, when the player has carrots and nothing else; deep floors put the hardest pressure on late monoculture. That pressure is where the cut Disease system's job went.

Selling `V` clean dollars of a good raises its `sat` by `V / SAT_DEPTH`. Nothing about rarity, freshness or bio appears in that expression — those are already inside `V`, so a $72 vodka batch moves the vodka bin exactly as far as $72 of carrots moves the carrot bin, and an heirloom pushes harder than a common. That is the whole of "denser items push down prices more", with no special case.

Recovery is **linear**, not exponential:

```
sat -= SAT_RECOVER_PER_DAY * dt / DAY_SECONDS      clamp at 0
```

Exponential would mean the last fifth never returns and the player lives permanently depressed. Linear gives *"potato is back in three days"*, which is plannable — and the player is planning plantings against it. `SAT_RECOVER_PER_DAY` **is** the design statement: it names the rotation width.

Recovery ticks every `dt` on every `StallGoodId`, whether or not the bin holds stock. It does not reset at the seam.

## Sell all integrates across the sale

If pressure landed after the sale, dumping 100 potatoes in one click would beat ten clicks of ten. Integrate. Price is linear in `sat`, so the exact result is a trapezoid — no loop, no chunking:

```
k    = 1 - SAT_FLOOR[good]
paid = V * (1 - k * (sat + V / (2 * SAT_DEPTH)))
```

Piecewise where `sat` would cross 1: integrate to `v* = SAT_DEPTH * (1 - sat)`, then pay the remainder flat at `SAT_FLOOR[good]`. Dumping and dribbling become identical, which removes the degenerate play before it can exist.

Order of operations: `marketGain` computes each good's clean subtotal exactly as it does today — freshness, `stallX`, `raritySale`, saleswoman, heirloom, bio, jam floor — and saturation applies **last, per good**, over that subtotal. Clearance's freshness-0 `$1` floor is exempt; it is already a floor.

Saturation is sampled at **Sell all**, never at consign. Consign keeps accumulating `worth` untouched.

## Legibility is the risk, not the maths

An unexplained shrinking number reads as the game being mean. Per [[ui/callout-hover]], a depressed price explains itself:

- Per-good row in [[ui/market]]: count, price index `%`, direction, days to clean.
- Sell all shows the paid total and the clean total it was measured against.
- Hover on a row: the callout names the floor and the recovery date.

`MarketQuote` and `SellAllQuote` are the shapes the panel reads. `World.marketQuote()` returns them; the panel does no arithmetic.

## What the player is supposed to do

Not wait — freshness rots held stock, so waiting is punished twice. Three answers, all of which must be legible from the panel:

- **Rotate.** Recovery rate names the rotation width.
- **Process.** Bins are decoupled: potato saturated, vodka bin clean. This is the strongest incentive in the feature. Processed goods do **not** inherit their input's saturation.
- **Contract.** Part 2.

# Part 2 — Contracts

## Board

Six offers, reset at the seam. Accepting takes an offer off today's board. Unaccepted offers vanish at the next seam — use it or lose it, and the decision lands in the recap ritual.

At most `CONTRACT_ACTIVE` (3) accepted at once.

**The board is `world + day + seed`. It reads no player state at all.** Not inventory, not plantings, not research. Every generator that reads player state is gamable: reading inventory means planting one seed to shift the board; reading research means *withholding* research to keep the pool narrow and the amounts in a crop you have industrialised. Both are achieved by not playing, which is the worst kind of exploit. The board is a world fact, like weather.

The day-one-all-vanilla problem is solved inside the budget instead — see `GOOD_COST`. Exotic goods cost difficulty, low-difficulty slots cannot afford them, and difficulty ramps with the day.

RNG stream is a new `SpatialId` `'contract'`, keyed `(day, slot, k)`. Spatial, not `Seq` — nothing is consumed, so the board needs no saved RNG state and regenerating it is free.

`ContractId = day * CONTRACT_SLOT_MAX + slot`. Derived, stable, no counter to save or digest. Board size is `CONTRACT_OFFERS + brokerTier`, and because slot `i` is deterministic in `(day, i)`, picking `broker` mid-day reveals new cards **without disturbing the ones already shown**.

Saved contract state is therefore only `Contracts`: `active`, `takenToday`, `history`, `book`. `takenToday` clears at the seam.

## Generator

### Difficulty is a budget, not a multiplier

Six free rolls give six mediocre boards or six impossible ones. Instead:

1. **Ladder the slots.** `SLOT_BANDS[i]` gives slot `i` a `[min, max]` difficulty window. Every board carries something takeable *and* something aspirational, by construction. `SLOT_BANDS` is sized `CONTRACT_SLOT_MAX` so the two `broker` slots have bands too.
2. **Ramp with the day.** `D` is drawn inside the slot band, then capped by `DIFFICULTY_START + DIFFICULTY_PER_DAY * day`, then clamped to `DIFFICULTY_MAX` (40). Displayed as `Stars` via `STAR_MIN`; the 0..40 integer stays internal.
3. **Pick a company.** Among companies eligible at `D`.
4. **Split the budget.** `Dmix = D * company.mix`, `Dval = D - Dmix`.

**`mix` is the company's dial, not a roll.** That is what stops mixins from starving value to zero — the failure you flagged where `any-of-type` plus a tight deadline eats the whole budget and leaves a rare+ order worth nothing. With a per-company split, `Dval > 0` always.

It is also what gives companies mechanical identity rather than flavour text: high `mix` reads as boutique (nasty requirements, small amounts), low `mix` reads as bulk (plain requirements, huge amounts). And the company then *explains* why a card looks the way it does, which is what stops the difficulty roll from feeling random.

### Demand is a grammar, dependent by construction

A flat mixin table implies independence that is not there — `any-of-type` only means something for jams and spirits, and rarity only means something for goods that carry it. So the good class is the first decision and it licenses the rest. `Demand` and `Lines` encode this:

- `Lines` is a tuple union of length 1 or 2. A pair is two `Demand`s. `Demand` has no recursive arm, so `two_specific_items<any_of_type<jam>, …>` is **unrepresentable**, not merely disallowed.
- `RarityGoodId` / `PlainGoodId` re-partition `StallGoodId` by whether it carries rarity — crops, spirits and wine do; sugar, jams, oil, flour and extract do not, per [[mechanics/market]]. A rarity requirement on a jam does not typecheck.
- `GroupId` is `'jam' | 'spirit'` only. Those are the two multi-member classes, which is exactly "in case of jams or liquors."

Mixed classes across a pair stay legal — `10 jars of strawberry jam and 10 L of vodka` is reachable; it just costs two good-selections plus `PAIR_COST`.

### Spending `Dmix`

Spend in this order, taking each option only if the remaining budget covers it, cheapest fallback otherwise:

1. Good from the company's pool → `GOOD_COST[good]`.
2. `GROUP_COST` (negative — a group is easier) if the good's class is `'jam'` or `'spirit'`.
3. `RARITY_COST[minRarity]`, only for a `rated` or `spirit`-group line.
4. `DEADLINE_COST[band]`.
5. `PAIR_COST` for a second line, which then repeats steps 1–3 for itself.

Leftover `Dmix` rolls back into `Dval`.

`GOOD_COST` carries crop tier. That is the mechanism that keeps vanilla off a day-one board without reading player state: low-`D` slots cannot afford it.

### Amount is derived, never rolled

100 L of vodka is not 100 potatoes. Amount comes out of value:

```
V      = VALUE_BASE * (1 + Dval / VALUE_SCALE)
unit   = clean stall price of the demand at its minRarity
amount = nice(V / unit)
```

`nice()` snaps down to `NICE_AMOUNTS`. A pair splits `V` between its two lines. A 100-potato contract and a 14-vodka contract are then the same contract, and markup becomes trivially statable.

### Deadline bounds amount, not the other way round

Bands are `DEADLINE_DAYS`: tight 1–2, normal 2–3, long 4–5, as you specified.

Against real grow times (`CROPS.growSeconds / DAY_SECONDS`) these are well calibrated. Carrot 0.38 d, potato 0.5, wheat 0.75, sugar-cane 0.83, watermelon 1.08, tomato 1.17, grape 1.25, raspberry 1.42, olive 1.5, vanilla 2.0. So:

- **`long` is a planting order for everything**, including vanilla with room for a still batch (`STILL_SECONDS` 0.75 d).
- **`normal` is a planting order for the fast crops** and a stock order for the slow ones.
- **`tight` is a stock order, always.** That is deliberate — it is what finally gives the freezer a job. You hold stock in order to be *able* to say yes.
- **Wine is stock-only at every band.** `BARREL_AGE` is 3 days on top of grow and press.

Because the window is short, amount is clamped by it:

```
amount = min(nice(V / unit), FEASIBLE_PER_DAY[good] * days * scale(day))
scale(day) = min(1, SCALE_START + day / SCALE_DAYS)
```

If the clamp drops amount below `AMOUNT_MIN`, discard the offer and re-roll that slot at the band minimum.

### Reward, penalty, cancel

```
markup  = MARKUP_BASE + MARKUP_PER_DIFFICULTY * D + MARKUP_PER_DAY * days
clean   = sum over lines of amount * unit
reward  = clean * (1 + markup)
penalty = PENALTY_RATE * clean
```

`PENALTY_RATE` sits in the 15–25% band you named.

The duration term is load-bearing. With 3 active slots the scarce resource is **slot-days**. Without it, five 2-day contracts strictly dominate one 5-day one and the planting orders — the point of the feature — become dead content. **Tune on markup per slot-day, never markup per contract.**

## Accepting, delivering, resolving

### Accept

`Act.acceptContract` (`'K'`). Legal iff research `unlock-contracts` done, `active.length < CONTRACT_ACTIVE + broker bonus`, the id is on today's board, and it is not in `takenToday`. Creates an `Active` with `dueDay = nowDay + offer.days` and one `Bin` per line at `filled: 0`.

`nowDay = (clock.day - 1) + clock.t / DAY_SECONDS`. The deadline runs from acceptance, not publication.

### Deliver

Delivery is the existing consign at the truck — no new act, and it stays logged as the `enqueue` cmd it already is. `consignBody` changes: a delivered stack fills contract bins **first**, then the stall remainder.

**Strict player order.** `contracts.active` array order *is* the fill priority; `Act.reorderContract` (`'O'`) moves an entry. No cleverness, no lookahead, no worst-first — the player sees which bin will take the drop before they drop it. Same call as the earlier kill of greedy allocation, for the same reason: a greedy filler cannot know the last apple is reserved for the megaorder.

Rules:

- A bin accepts a unit iff `Accepts(demand, good, rarity)` and `filled < amount`. Rarity is a **minimum**; higher qualifies.
- Over-qualified delivery counts as one unit at the contract rate. No overage bonus. Nothing is wasted, nothing is rewarded.
- Freshness-0 fruit does not count toward a bin. Otherwise freshness is ignored by contracts — the contract states units.
- A full bin passes through to the next bin, then to the stall.
- Contract-bound units **do not** enter `StallGood.worth` and **do not** raise `sat`.

The cheapest-qualifying-rarity rule belongs to a filler that chooses its own goods. There is no such filler in 1.8.0; it lands with silo-as-bin.

### Complete

Bins all full → `money += reward`, `book` `done += 1`, history entry `{ kind: 'done' }`, slot freed. Immediate, on the delivering tick.

### Miss

Resolved on the tick that crosses `dueDay`, not at the seam.

```
sold    = delivered units sold at the current saturated market rate
penalty = offer.penalty * max(PENALTY_FLOOR, 1 - filledFraction)
```

Both halves matter, and this is the fix for the break-even hole you found. **Paying market rate for what was delivered** stops a stretch contract from being a trap — you lose the markup, not the goods. **`PENALTY_FLOOR`** stops the near-miss from being free: with a bare `(1 - f)` scale, 99% delivered costs 1% of the penalty, nobody ever cancels, and the deadline stops meaning anything.

Those units go to the stall at the saturated rate and **do** raise `sat`. Completed contracts bypass the market; failed ones dump into it. A failed bulk contract therefore also tanks that good's price — a second, self-inflicted sting with no new mechanic behind it.

`book` `missed += 1`.

### Cancel

`Act.cancelContract` (`'X'`). Fee ramps to the miss penalty over the contract's life:

```
fee = lerp(CANCEL_MIN * clean, missPenalty(active), elapsed / days)
```

Flat cancel cost would make cancel-at-deadline strictly dominant and the miss penalty dead content. Ramping makes *deciding early* the skill. Delivered units are sold as in Miss. Cancel is not a miss: `book` is untouched, history records `{ kind: 'cancelled' }`.

## Proposed invariants

Move to [[mechanics/_index]] as numbered entries at implementation. Tests follow them.

1. `sat` is `0..1` per `StallGoodId`, starts 0, ticks down `SAT_RECOVER_PER_DAY` per day on every good every `dt`, never resets at the seam.
2. Sell all of clean value `V` at `sat` pays the trapezoid, clamped piecewise at `SAT_FLOOR[good]`. Ten sales of `V/10` pay the same total as one sale of `V`.
3. Saturation applies last, per good, over the existing `marketGain` subtotal. Clearance's `$1` floor is exempt.
4. Contract delivery raises no `sat` and enters no `StallGood.worth`. Miss and cancel remainders do both.
5. Board slot `i` on day `d` is a pure function of `(seed, d, i)`. Same seed, same day → same offer, regardless of inventory, plantings, research, money, or `clock.t`.
6. `ContractId = day * CONTRACT_SLOT_MAX + slot`. Growing the board with `broker` adds slots and does not change slots 0..5.
7. A `Demand` never carries a rarity for a `PlainGoodId`, and `Lines` never nests.
8. `amount >= AMOUNT_MIN` on every published offer, and `amount <= FEASIBLE_PER_DAY[good] * days * scale(day)`.
9. `reward = clean * (1 + markup)` baked at generation. Saturation at delivery time does not move it.
10. Miss pays market rate for delivered units and `offer.penalty * max(PENALTY_FLOOR, 1 - filled/need)`. `filled = need` is completion, never a miss.
11. Cancel fee at `elapsed = 0` is `CANCEL_MIN * clean`; at `elapsed = days` it equals the miss penalty at that fill.
12. Consign fills `active` in array order, then the stall. A full bin passes through.
13. Guest `acceptContract` / `cancelContract` / `reorderContract` never enter a bundle. Guest consign fills bins.
14. Board generation is not a `Cmd`.
15. `SAVE_VERSION` 1.8, `PROTOCOL` 1.8, wordmark 1.8.0. 1.72 file → `'version'`. Lands in 1.8.0-d, not a/b/c.
