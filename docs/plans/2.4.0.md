## Preface

One ranked field does nine jobs today: sale, grow time, tolerance, rot, stack identity, stall bins, still and barrel output, contract minimums, and one sale skill. It is the only place the player's care shows up, and because it is a ladder, every plant of a crop wants the same thing — the top of it.

2.4 cuts it in two.

**Variety** is what the plant *is*. Identity, not a ladder. It fixes how picky the plant is, how long it takes, and which of three end uses it is good for. Set when the seed goes in the ground and never changes after. Two varieties of the same crop are siblings, not steps.

**Quality** is how well the plant was *treated*. A percentage. It sets sale price, it rides along into the seed the fruit grinds down to, and it averages when stacks merge. Bought seed starts at nothing; a plant kept happy hands its seed a better start than it got.

The point of the pair: variety asks *what is this field for*, quality asks *how well am I running it*. Neither question has one right answer, which is what the ladder never allowed.

Three plants get nothing — carrot has nowhere to be processed, vanilla goes one place and is already the fussiest crop on the farm, and a premium bulk crop is a contradiction. Olive gets two varieties and no heirloom: table olive and oil olive is a real split, but neither is a prize. Grape and apricot are the only crops with three genuine end uses, so they are the only two carrying a full row.

Out of 2.4, into the next: how varieties are earned. No research rows, no shop path, no contract demand for them. The player starts owning one pack of everything so the mechanic can be played end to end while the way in is still missing.

## Variety

```
VarietyTier = 'base' | 'variant' | 'heirloom'
VarietyId   = 'base' | 'bintje' | 'russian-banana' | 'sonora' | 'red-fife'
            | 'green-zebra' | 'san-marzano' | 'black-raspberry'
            | 'concord' | 'thompson' | 'keknyelu'
            | 'kingston-black' | 'pink-lady'
            | 'moorpark' | 'klosterneuburger' | 'blenheim'
            | 'kalamata' | 'arbequina' | 'montmorency' | 'bing'
```

`'base'` is legal on every `CropId`. Every other id belongs to exactly one crop. `VARIETY: Record<Exclude<VarietyId, 'base'>, { crop: CropId; tier: 'variant' | 'heirloom'; use: Use; gimmick?: never }>` and `VARIETIES: Record<CropId, readonly VarietyId[]>`, both complete maps, `defs/varieties.ts`. Carrot, vanilla and sugar-cane list `['base']` only.

`Plant.variety`, `Tree.variety`, and the `variety` field on seeds, fruit and grafts are all required `VarietyId`. Illegal: optional `variety`. Illegal: a `variety` whose `VARIETY[v].crop` is not the item's `crop`.

### Numbers

`VARIETY_GROW`, `VARIETY_TOL`, `VARIETY_ROT` — `Record<VarietyTier, number>`, transposed from today's table: `base` takes the common row, `heirloom` the heirloom row, `variant` the **average of the uncommon and rare rows**. Tuned-to the old table, so a base plant grows and drinks exactly as a common one did.

`statsOf(crop, variety, quality, mods)`:

- sale: `CROPS.sale × qualityMul(quality) × RATING_SALE[use.fresh] × Π saleMul`
- grow: `(CROPS.growSeconds × VARIETY_GROW[tier]) / growSpeed`
- drink: unchanged
- tols: `tolerance(base, tier) = max(TOL_MIN, base × VARIETY_TOL[tier])`
- rot: `CROPS.rotSeconds × VARIETY_ROT[tier]`

Vanilla keeps its own `saleMul` preference; it has one variety, so that is now a flat number.

### Use

```
Rating = 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5
Use    = { preserve: Rating | 'none'; fresh: Rating; alcohol: Rating | 'none' }
```

Three paths. **preserve** is the jam machine and the mill. **fresh** is the fruit sold as it is. **alcohol** is the still and the barrel. `'none'` means the crop has no such machine at all — not a bad rating, an absent one. Base is `3` wherever a path exists.

`RATING_SALE: Record<Rating, number>` — `1` 0.6, `2` 0.8, `3` 1, `4` 1.3, `5` 1.8 — preference. Neutral at 3, so a crop with no varieties is untouched by this whole section. It multiplies the sale of the good made on that path, and nothing else: not yield, not speed, not input count.

## Quality

`quality: number`, 0..1, required on `Plant`, seeds, fruit, graft, spirit, cask, jam, oil, flour, extract and sugar. Illegal: optional `quality`. Grass extract and shop sugar are `0`.

`qualityMul(q) = 1 + (QUALITY_TOP − 1) × q`. `QUALITY_TOP` is tuned-to the old heirloom sale row, so **`qualityMul(0)` is exactly today's common price** and nothing inflates on day one.

Bought seed is `0`. Tree fruit is `0` — trees have no happiness, so they have no source for it, and this keeps every tree at today's numbers.

### At ripen

No roll. `rollGrowRarity`, `ripenN`, `RARITY_WEIGHT` and the `grow` stream are gone — [[mechanics/rng]] loses a stream and a counter.

```
quality = clamp(seed.quality + qualityGain(happiness) + betterGain, 0, 1)
```

`qualityGain(h)` is piecewise linear: `+QUALITY_STEP` at `HAPPY_MAX`, `0` at `HAPPY_START`, `−QUALITY_STEP` at `0`. `QUALITY_STEP` 0.25 — preference. A plant left at the happiness it was planted with hands its seed exactly what it was given; four clean generations reach the top, and a neglected one walks back down.

`betterGain` is `BETTER_QUALITY × tier × (h / HAPPY_MAX)` when the player owns `better-{crop}`, else 0. That is `BETTER_UP1` transposed — the skill still rewards care, it just pays in the new currency.

### Stacks and bins

Countable items merge on kind and identity as before, with **`variety` joining the identity key** and `quality` joining the weighted average beside `unitSale` and `freshness`. Different variety never merges. Same variety at different quality merges and averages, weighted by count — by liters for sugar.

Stall crop bins are per crop × variety × organic. Consign folds both per-unit properties in at the truck, the way freshness already worked:

```
worth += count × freshMul(freshness) × qualityMul(quality) × RATING_SALE[use.fresh]
```

`raritySale` leaves `marketGain` entirely. What is left there is `stallX`, the sale skills, weather and saturation — [[mechanics/market]].

## Sprint 2 — Propagation

How a variety moves from one plot to another. At the end you can make more of what you have, and the heirlooms behave like themselves.

### Grafts

```
{ kind: 'graft'; crop: CropId; variety: VarietyId; quality: number; count: number }
```

Stacks on crop + variety, quality averaged. Not compost. Furnace takes it at the green rate.

**A graft is never planted. It always attaches.** New ground comes from seed; a graft changes what is already growing there.

Two sources:

- **Axe on a mature tree** — 1 wood and **2 grafts of that tree's variety**, then the existing chop result: `trunk = true`, `juvenile = 0`, fruit lost. Chopping is now how an orchard reproduces, which finally gives tree varieties a source — [[mechanics/trees]] `trees.chop`.
- **Research station** — heirloom fruit in, fruit and grafts out. Sprint 4.

`Intent` `{ act: 'graft'; at: Coord }`. `dest` = `at`. Hold a graft. Work `GRAFT_WORK` — preference. Prompt **Graft {graftable} to {graftable target}** 

Legal targets, same crop, graft's tier is anything, **target's variety tier is not heirloom**:

| target         | state                                                                                                                                                 |
| -------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| annual `Plant` | `growing`. Not ripe, dead, rotten, empty.                                                                                                             |
| `Tree`         | `juvenile < 1` — that is a sapling or a `trunk`. Not mature. Message if tree is grown "I cannot graft to a fully grown tree - I need to cut it first" |

Complete: the target's `variety` becomes the graft's, the target's `quality` becomes the graft's, one graft is consumed. Maturity, juvenile progress, `trunk`, happiness, `tended`, organic and the soil are untouched. An heirloom cannot be grafted over.

### Grinder

Two changes, one rule: **the grinder returns the plainest thing that fruit can be.**

- Annual base or variant fruit → seeds of the same crop and variety. Unchanged but for the field name.
- Annual heirloom fruit → seeds of the same crop at `'base'`.
- For fruits variant-1 and variant-2, it drops out same variant.
- **Tree fruit is now accepted** and yields `{ kind: 'tree-seed' }` of that species at `'base'`, whatever went in. The refuse list loses tree fruit and keeps sugar. This is the only way to make a tree seed outside a contract prize, and it is what makes the base apple you need before you can graft anything onto it.

Seed quality equals the fruit's quality in every case. The hopper locks crop + variety.

Quality raises the floor of the yield: `GRIND_MIN_AT(q) = GRIND_MIN + round((GRIND_MAX − GRIND_MIN) × q)`, rolled up to `GRIND_MAX`. At full quality the roll is gone and you always get the maximum.

### Needs a neighbour

`keknyelu`, `pink-lady`, `bing`. All three are genuinely self-incompatible, and all three sit on a crop that has a non-heirloom sibling to stand next to.

`NEIGHBOUR_REACH` 2 — preference. Chebyshev, from the plot, or from either cell of a 1×2 tree. shown outline on hover-over, green if satisfied, red if not.

A cell in reach is a valid neighbour when it holds:

- an annual of the same crop, variety tier not heirloom, `growing`, with neither the water band nor the fertilizer band red; or
- a tree of the same species, variety tier not heirloom, `juvenile >= 1`, `trunk === false`.

A sapling, a trunk, a ripe plant, a starving one, a dead or rotten one, and another heirloom are all not neighbours.

Without one, the plant does not advance toward fruit:

- annual: `maturity` does not increase. Water, fertilizer, happiness, stunt and death all still tick — a lonely plant can still die of thirst.
- tree: `fruit` does not increase and the seam does not turn `pending` into `on`. `juvenile` still grows, so a lone orchard can be raised and then given its neighbour.

Look line and inspect name it — [[ui/inspect]]. Sprint 4 writes the copy.

## Sprint 3 — Value chain

Where the ratings pay. At the end the three columns of the table mean three different farms.

**Every machine carries quality and rating onto what it makes.** Output quality is the mean of what went in; output sale takes `RATING_SALE` of the input variety's rating on that machine's path. Jam and the mill ignored both before, which is exactly how a good tomato became an anonymous jar.

| machine | lock | output sale |
|---|---|---|
| mill | crop + variety | product × `RATING_SALE[preserve]` × `qualityMul(mean q)` |
| jam | crop + variety | `JAM_SALE[crop]` × `RATING_SALE[preserve]` × `qualityMul(mean q)` |
| still | none | see below |
| barrel | crop + variety | `CASK_SALE[cask]` × `RATING_SALE[alcohol]` × `qualityMul(q)` × age |
| grinder | crop + variety | seeds, sprint 2 |
| furnace, compost | none | unchanged, flat rate, variety and quality ignored |

**Still.** No lock, it still mixes. On finish: if every unit shares one crop **and** one variety, the batch is that crop's named spirit at that variety — `SPIRIT_SALE[spirit] × RATING_SALE[alcohol] × qualityMul(mean q)`. Anything else is `mixed`, at `SPIRIT_SALE.vodka × MIXED_MUL × qualityMul(mean q)` with a neutral rating. One variety or mixed; there is no partial credit.

**Barrel.** `caskAgeTop(q)` replaces `CASK_AGE[rarity]`, lerped over quality. `caskAgeOf` still reads the multiplier back out of `unitSale`.
to do: named specialty alcohols.

**Cut fruit.** `{ kind: 'fruit'; ...; cut: boolean }`, required, `false` from the field. The research station returns the fruit it took with `cut = true` and refuses fruit that already carries it. Cut fruit is otherwise ordinary — it sells, jams and stills identically. Illegal: optional `cut`.

**Named jams.** Jam locks a variety, so the jar knows what went into it. Five varieties have a real product of their own and get a name; every other variety falls back to the plain jam of its crop, and base tomato keeps **Ketchup**.

| variety | jam reads |

| `black-raspberry` | Black raspberry jam |
| `montmorency` | Sour cherry preserve |
| `blenheim` | Blenheim apricot jam |
| `san-marzano` | Passata |

`sim/recipe.ts` is still the one enumeration: rows gain the variety they are pinned to, `recipesUsing` matches on crop + variety for a `one` input, and the almanac Ingredients list follows without a hardcoded plate. Catalog `clockText` stays nominal.
